# Storing Coffee Beans

## The four enemies of fresh coffee
Air (oxygen), light, heat and moisture all make coffee go stale. Good storage limits all four.

## Best everyday storage
- Keep beans in an airtight, opaque container, or in the original bag if it has a valve and seals well. Squeeze the air out before closing.
- Store at room temperature in a cool, dark cupboard, away from the stove, oven, dishwasher and sunny windows.
- Use opened beans within about 2 to 4 weeks for best flavor.
- Grind only what you need, right before brewing.

## Should I keep coffee in the fridge?
No. The refrigerator is humid, the beans absorb odors from other foods, and moving them in and out causes condensation. Room temperature in a sealed container is better.

## Freezing coffee
Freezing is fine for long-term storage of beans you will not use within a few weeks:
1. Divide beans into airtight portions of about one week's use each.
2. Freeze them in freezer bags or jars with as little air as possible.
3. Take out one portion at a time and let it reach room temperature before opening, so moisture does not condense on the beans.
4. Do not refreeze a portion after it has thawed.
Frozen beans keep good flavor for a couple of months.

## Ground coffee
Ground coffee goes stale much faster than whole beans. If you buy it pre-ground, store it airtight and use it within about 1 to 2 weeks.

## Signs coffee is stale
Weak or little aroma when you open the container, flat or papery or cardboard-like taste, and very little bloom (bubbling) when hot water hits fresh grounds. Stale coffee is safe to drink, it just tastes worse. Mold, which is rare and usually caused by moisture, means you should throw it out.
