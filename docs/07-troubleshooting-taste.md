# Troubleshooting: Why Does My Coffee Taste Like That?

## Sour, tangy or salty
Usually **under-extracted**. Fixes, one at a time:
- Grind finer.
- Use hotter water (195°F to 205°F, 90°C to 96°C).
- Brew longer, or for espresso, let more liquid come out (a longer ratio).
- Check that very light roasts are brewed hot and fine enough.
Pleasant, juicy or fruity acidity is different from harsh sourness; bright light roasts are supposed to have some.

## Bitter, harsh, burnt or dry in the mouth
Usually **over-extracted**, or the beans are very dark. Fixes:
- Grind coarser.
- Shorten the brew time (and for French press, pour off the coffee right after pressing).
- Lower the water temperature slightly.
- Do not leave coffee on a drip machine's hot plate.
- Try a lighter roast if dark roasts always taste burnt to you.
- Clean the brewer and grinder; rancid oils taste bitter.

## Weak or watery
- Use more coffee (a stronger ratio such as 1:15 or 1:14).
- Grind a little finer.
- Make sure all water passes through the coffee and not around it (in drip machines, check the filter sits properly).

## Too strong
Use less coffee or add hot water after brewing. Adding water to finished coffee is perfectly fine and keeps the flavor balanced.

## Both bitter and sour at once
Usually an **uneven grind** (a blade grinder or a worn burr grinder) or uneven brewing, so some grounds over-extract while others under-extract. Use a burr grinder, pour evenly, and for pour-over make sure all grounds are wet during the bloom.

## Flat, dull, papery or stale
- Beans are too old. Check the roast date and buy fresher.
- Coffee was ground too long before brewing.
- Rinse paper filters before brewing.
- Avoid distilled water; it makes coffee taste flat.

## Muddy or gritty
- French press: grind coarser, scoop the crust off before pressing, press gently, and leave the last bit in the pot.
- Use a paper filter for a cleaner cup.

## Chemical, chlorine or metallic taste
Water is the usual cause. Use filtered water, and descale your machine or kettle.

## Espresso specifics
- **Runs too fast, pale and sour**: grind finer, check dose and tamp evenly.
- **Runs too slow or drips, dark and bitter**: grind coarser.
- **Spurts or sprays from one side (channeling)**: improve distribution and tamp level.
- **No crema**: beans are stale or the grind is too coarse. Very fresh beans make more crema.

## Coffee upsets my stomach
Try darker roasts or low-acid origins (Brazil, Sumatra), cold brew, or adding a little milk. Drinking coffee with food helps many people. If symptoms are persistent, check with a doctor.
