# Choosing a Coffee You Will Like: Roast, Origin and Process

## Roast levels
- **Light roast**: light brown, dry surface. Bright acidity, fruity or floral flavors, tea-like body, the most origin character. Best for pour-over, drip and AeroPress. Caffeine per bean is about the same as dark roast; light-roasted beans are denser, so a cup measured by weight has roughly the same caffeine.
- **Medium roast**: medium brown. Balanced sweetness and acidity with caramel, nut and chocolate notes. Works well for almost every brew method and is the safest choice if you are unsure.
- **Medium-dark roast**: darker brown, sometimes small oil spots. Fuller body, chocolate and toasted flavors, less acidity. Good for espresso, moka pot and coffee with milk.
- **Dark roast** (often labeled French, Italian or espresso roast): dark brown to nearly black, oily surface. Smoky, bitter-sweet, roasty flavors; low acidity; heavy body. Holds up well to milk and sugar. Oily beans can clog some grinders and automatic machines.

Names like "breakfast blend" or "house blend" are not standardized; check the roast description or tasting notes instead.

## Flavor by origin (general tendencies)
- **Ethiopia**: floral (jasmine), citrus, berries, bergamot; light body. Natural-processed Ethiopians are intensely fruity, like blueberry or strawberry.
- **Kenya**: bold, juicy acidity, blackcurrant, grapefruit, tomato-like savory sweetness.
- **Colombia**: balanced and approachable; caramel, red apple, mild citrus, chocolate. A great first single origin.
- **Brazil**: low acidity, nutty, chocolate, peanut, heavy body. A common espresso base.
- **Guatemala**: chocolate, toffee, spice, gentle acidity.
- **Costa Rica**: clean, sweet, bright, honey and citrus.
- **Central America in general** (Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua): sweet, balanced, caramel and fruit.
- **Sumatra and Indonesia**: earthy, herbal, cedar, dark chocolate, very low acidity, heavy body.
- **Peru, Mexico**: mild, nutty, soft chocolate, easy everyday coffee.

These are tendencies. Farm, variety, processing and roast change the result a lot.

## Processing methods
After harvest, the coffee seed has to be separated from the cherry fruit. The method changes flavor:
- **Washed (wet)**: fruit removed before drying. Clean, crisp, bright, shows the origin clearly.
- **Natural (dry)**: whole cherry dried with the seed inside. Fruity, winey, sweet, heavier body, sometimes fermented or boozy notes.
- **Honey (pulped natural)**: some fruit left on during drying. In between: sweet, rounded, mild fruit.
- **Wet-hulled**: common in Sumatra. Earthy, heavy, low acidity.
- **Anaerobic or experimental fermentations**: intense, unusual flavors such as tropical fruit, cinnamon or wine. Exciting but polarizing.

## Matching coffee to your taste
- **"I like it smooth, chocolatey, not sour"**: medium to medium-dark roast from Brazil, Colombia, Guatemala or a chocolatey blend. Washed or honey process.
- **"I like it bold and strong"**: medium-dark or dark roast; Sumatra or an espresso blend. Note that "strong" in taste mostly comes from the ratio of coffee to water, not from the roast.
- **"I want fruity, bright, interesting"**: light roast Ethiopian or Kenyan; try a natural process for berry flavors.
- **"I drink it with milk and sugar"**: medium-dark or dark roast, Brazil or espresso blends, which keep their flavor through milk.
- **"I drink it black"**: light or medium roast lets you taste the bean's sweetness without bitterness.
- **"Coffee upsets my stomach"**: try darker roasts or low-acid origins such as Brazil or Sumatra, and avoid very light roasts.

## Matching coffee to your brew method
- **Drip machine, pour-over**: light to medium roast single origins or blends.
- **French press**: medium to dark roast; its heavy body suits chocolatey, nutty coffees.
- **Espresso**: blends or single origins labeled for espresso; medium to dark roasts are easiest to dial in.
- **Moka pot**: medium to dark roast.
- **Cold brew**: medium to dark roast, chocolatey and nutty origins such as Brazil or Colombia. Very light roasts can taste sour or thin as cold brew.
- **AeroPress**: anything; it is very forgiving.
