# How to Brew: Step-by-Step Methods

## Automatic drip coffee machine
**You need:** medium grind, about 1:16 ratio (for example 60 g coffee for a 1 liter / 34 oz pot, or 2 level tablespoons per 6 oz cup), a paper or permanent filter.
1. Fill the reservoir with fresh, cold filtered water.
2. Rinse a paper filter with hot water if you can, to remove papery taste.
3. Add ground coffee and level the bed with a gentle shake.
4. Start the machine.
5. Remove the pot from the hot plate when brewing finishes and drink within about 30 minutes, or move it to a thermal carafe. A hot plate makes coffee taste burnt over time.
Tip: machines certified by the Specialty Coffee Association reach proper brewing temperature; cheap machines often brew too cool, which makes coffee sour. If yours does, use a slightly finer grind.

## Pour-over (V60, Kalita Wave, Melitta cone)
**You need:** 15 g coffee, 250 g water at about 200°F (93°C), medium-fine grind, paper filter, a kettle (gooseneck preferred), a scale and a timer.
1. Rinse the paper filter with hot water and discard the rinse water.
2. Add the ground coffee and make a small dip in the center.
3. **Bloom:** start the timer and pour about 30 to 45 g of water to wet all the grounds. Wait 30 to 45 seconds.
4. Pour slowly in small circles, avoiding the filter walls, until the scale reads 150 g by about 1:15.
5. Pour the rest to reach 250 g by about 1:45.
6. Give the brewer a gentle swirl so the bed is flat. Let it drain.
7. Total brew time should be about 2:30 to 3:30. Faster: grind finer. Slower: grind coarser.

## Chemex
Use the same steps as pour-over with a medium-coarse grind, a Chemex bonded filter (three-layer side toward the spout), and a larger dose such as 30 g coffee to 500 g water. Target total time 3:30 to 4:30.

## French press
**You need:** coarse grind, about 1:15 ratio (30 g coffee to 450 g water for a medium press), water just off the boil.
1. Preheat the press with hot water and discard it.
2. Add coffee, then pour all the water, making sure all grounds are wet.
3. Put the lid on with the plunger up. Steep 4 minutes.
4. Stir or break the crust on top, scoop off the foam and floating grounds with a spoon (optional, makes a cleaner cup).
5. Press the plunger down slowly and gently.
6. Pour all the coffee out right away into cups or a carafe. Coffee left sitting on the grounds keeps extracting and turns bitter.
Tip: if your cup is muddy, use a coarser, more even grind and pour gently, leaving the last bit in the press. For an even cleaner cup, steep longer (up to 8 to 9 minutes) without stirring, then press only to the surface of the liquid.

## AeroPress
**Simple standard method:** 15 g coffee, medium-fine grind, 220 g water at about 185°F to 195°F (85°C to 90°C).
1. Put a paper filter in the cap and rinse it. Attach the cap to the chamber and set it on a sturdy mug.
2. Add coffee, start a timer, and pour water to the top mark or 220 g.
3. Stir for about 10 seconds. Insert the plunger slightly to create a seal, which stops dripping.
4. At 1:30 to 2:00, remove the plunger, stir again (optional), reinsert and press gently for about 20 to 30 seconds until you hear a hiss. Stop at the hiss.
The AeroPress is very forgiving: shorter times and finer grinds make it more concentrated; you can also make a concentrate and add hot water.

## Moka pot (stovetop espresso maker)
**You need:** fine to medium-fine grind (coarser than espresso), medium to dark roast.
1. Fill the bottom chamber with hot water up to just below the safety valve. Starting with hot water reduces the time the pot sits on the stove and avoids a burnt taste.
2. Fill the funnel basket with coffee, level it, and **do not tamp** it down.
3. Wipe grounds off the rim and screw the pot together firmly, using a towel because the base is hot.
4. Heat on medium-low with the lid open.
5. When coffee flows out steadily, keep the heat low. When it turns pale and starts sputtering or gurgling, remove it from the heat immediately and run the base under cold water to stop extraction.
6. Serve as is, or dilute with hot water for an Americano-style cup or add steamed milk.
Never let the pot run dry, and never block or remove the safety valve.

## Espresso machine
**Standard starting recipe:** 18 g finely ground coffee in, about 36 g espresso out (a 1:2 ratio), in 25 to 30 seconds, at about 200°F (93°C).
1. Warm up the machine and portafilter fully (often 15 to 30 minutes for home machines).
2. Dose the coffee into the dry portafilter basket and distribute it evenly.
3. Tamp level and firm. Consistency matters more than force.
4. Flush the group head briefly, lock in the portafilter, and start the shot right away.
5. Stop when the cup reaches about 36 g.
**Dialing in:** shot runs too fast and tastes sour: grind finer. Shot runs too slow, chokes or tastes bitter: grind coarser. Adjust grind first and keep dose and output fixed while you learn.
Milk drinks: a latte is espresso with lots of steamed milk and a thin foam layer; a cappuccino has less milk and thicker foam; a flat white uses a double shot with a small amount of silky milk; an Americano is espresso diluted with hot water.

## Cold brew
**You need:** extra coarse grind, medium to dark roast, a jar or pitcher, a fine strainer and paper filter.
1. Combine coffee and cold or room-temperature water at 1:5 to 1:8 for concentrate (for example 100 g coffee to 600 g water).
2. Stir to wet all the grounds. Cover.
3. Steep 12 to 18 hours in the refrigerator, or 12 to 16 hours at room temperature.
4. Strain through a fine mesh strainer, then through a paper filter for a cleaner result.
5. Dilute the concentrate with water or milk, usually about 1:1, and serve over ice.
Cold brew concentrate keeps about 1 to 2 weeks in the refrigerator. It tastes smooth and low in acidity.

## Iced coffee (fast method, Japanese-style)
Brew pour-over with hot water directly onto ice so it chills instantly and keeps more aroma than cold brew. Replace about 40% of the brew water with ice in the carafe: for example 20 g coffee, 180 g hot water poured over 120 g ice, with a slightly finer grind than usual.

## Turkish coffee
Extra fine grind (like flour). Combine about 7 to 9 g coffee, 70 to 90 ml cold water and sugar if desired in a cezve. Heat slowly without stirring after the initial mix until foam rises toward the rim, then remove before it boils over. Pour gently into a small cup, keeping the foam, and let grounds settle before drinking.
